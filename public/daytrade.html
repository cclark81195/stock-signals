Research only. This page finds quick in-and-out setups and tests them against the last ~60 days of 5-minute prices, with trading costs included. It places no orders. Automation comes later, once the numbers hold up.

What every setting, number and column means. Hidden by default to save space.
    Pick up to 10. Every S&P 500 stock is available, plus SPY (the S&P 500 index fund) and the five biggest cryptocurrencies other than stablecoins: Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB, XRP and Solana. Very heavily traded names work best, because tight bid-ask spreads are what keep small edges alive.
    Buys when price closes above the high of the first 15 minutes of trading (before 11:30 ET) on strong volume and above VWAP. The stop sits at the bottom of that opening range.
    Buys when price climbs back above VWAP (the day's volume-weighted average price) after spending most of the last half hour below it. The stop sits just under the recent lows.
    Looks for a sharp rise, then a short, shallow, quieter pullback (3 to 8 bars that give back at most half the rise). Buys the first close above the pullback's high on a pickup in volume. The stop sits under the pullback's low.
    Looks for a flat ceiling touched at least twice in the last 2 hours while the lows keep rising. Buys the first close above the ceiling on strong volume. The stop sits under the most recent low.
    In an uptrend (price above VWAP, the 9-bar average above a rising 20-bar average), waits for a dip that tests the 20-bar average, then buys when a green bar closes back above the 9-bar average and the last high. The stop sits under the dip.
    After a clear up-move, waits for a pullback of 38% to 62% of that move that does not break the starting low, then buys the first green bar that closes above the last high. The stop sits just under the 78.6% level.
    Two lows within 0.25% of each other (6 to 30 bars apart) with a rally between them. Buys the first close above the middle peak on strong volume. The stop sits under the lows.
    For stocks that open at least 2% above yesterday's close and hold the gap. Buys the first close above the first 15 minutes' high (before 10:30 ET) on strong volume. Stocks only. It takes priority over the plain opening-range breakout when both are on.
    Skips a stock trade unless SPY is above its own VWAP at that moment, so you only buy when the whole market is leaning your way. Stocks only; crypto ignores it.
    Skips a trade unless the stock's volume in the first 30 minutes is at least 1.2 times its recent average. Unusual volume means something is happening, and breakouts work better then.
    Fixed uses your profit target for every setup. Measured move aims for the height of the pattern (the rise before the flag, or the triangle's height), kept between 1R and 4R. Opening-range and VWAP trades always use the fixed target.
    No new buys between 11:30 AM and 2:00 PM ET, when trading is often slow and choppy. Stocks only; crypto ignores it.
    When a trade reaches a profit equal to the amount risked (+1R), sells half and moves the stop on the rest to the buy price. This wins more often and gives up some of the big winners. With whole shares, a one-share trade cannot be split.

Triangles pointing up are buys and triangles pointing down are sells; a sell is green when the trade made money and red when it lost.     Latest session

    Symbol Status Details Entry Stop Target Shares
    What these columns and statuses mean
    Status
    Signal: a setup just triggered on the last finished 5-minute bar, and the Entry, Stop, Target and Shares show the plan. In trade: a simulated position is still open. Traded: the rules already took and closed trades in this session. Waiting: the opening range is still forming. No setup: nothing has triggered. No data / Error: prices could not be loaded for this symbol.
    Details
    A plain-English reason for the status, such as which setup fired and why.
    Entry
    The planned buy price, after slippage. A signal is read when a bar closes, and the buy happens at the next bar's open, so the real price can differ a little.
    Stop
    The price where the trade is closed at a loss. Placed before entering, so the most you can lose is known in advance.
    Target
    The price where the trade is closed at a profit: the entry plus your chosen multiple of the risk.
    Shares
    How many shares the plan buys, or how many units for crypto, which can be a fraction such as 0.0123. It is your risk amount divided by the distance from Entry to Stop, capped so the position never exceeds your account size.

    By symbol

    Symbol Trades Win % Net R / trade Profit factor Total R Max drawdown
    What these columns mean
    Trades
    How many simulated trades this symbol produced. Fewer than about 100 is too few to draw conclusions from.
    Win %
    The share of trades that made money after costs. A low win rate can still work if wins are bigger than losses.
    Net R / trade
    The average result of one trade after costs, in R. Positive means the rules made money on average. This is the most important column.
    Profit factor
    Total won divided by total lost. Above 1 means winners outweigh losers; below 1 means the rules lost money.
    Total R
    All trades added up, after costs, in R.
    Max drawdown
    The largest fall from a high point in the running total. It shows how deep a bad stretch got, so ask whether you could stick with the plan through it.

    All simulated trades

    When (ET) Symbol Setup Buy price Sell price Shares Exit reason Result P&L
    What these columns and exit reasons mean
    When (ET)
    The day and time the trade was entered, in New York time.
    Buy price / Sell price
    The price per share paid and received. Both include slippage where it applies, so they are slightly worse than the raw market price. If half was sold early (the "Sell half at +1R" option), the sell price is the average of the two sales.
    Shares
    How many shares (or units of crypto) were bought and then sold. With "Dollars per buy" filled in, it is that amount divided by the buy price; otherwise it is sized so that hitting the stop loses about your chosen risk.
    Exit reason
    Target: price reached the profit target. Stop: price hit the stop. Gap through stop: price opened below the stop, so the exit was at the open and the loss is larger than planned. End-of-day close: still open at the cutoff (3:45 PM ET for stocks, 23:45 UTC for crypto), so it was closed. ½ at 1R next to a reason means half the shares were sold at +1R first, and the reason is how the other half ended. Session end: the data ended before the cutoff, such as on a half day.
    Result
    The outcome after costs in R. +2R is twice the amount risked; −1R is a full loss of the amount risked. It can be a bit below −1R after slippage or a gap.
    P&L
    Profit or loss in dollars: (sell price − buy price) × shares, after costs. The last row of the table adds them all up.

    How the strategy and the safety rules work

    Opening-range breakout. The first 15 minutes set a high and a low. If a 5-minute bar closes above the high before 11:30 ET, on more than 1.5x the day's average volume and above VWAP, the system buys at the next bar's open. The stop is the bottom of the opening range.

    VWAP reclaim. After 10:00 ET, if price spent most of the last six bars below VWAP and then a green bar closes back above it on decent volume, it buys at the next open. The stop sits just under the last four bars' lows.

    Exits. A profit target at the chosen multiple of risk, the stop, or a flat-by time of 3:45 PM ET, whichever comes first. If one bar touches both stop and target, the test assumes the stop hit first.

    Risk rules (the same ones a live bot would run). Each trade risks a fixed percentage of the account. The system stops for the day after 3 trades, after losing 2R, or after 2 losses in a row. Stops that are tighter than normal 5-minute noise, or wider than 1% of price, are skipped. No leverage by default.

    Costs. Slippage is charged on every entry and stop or time exit. The gap between "before costs" and "after costs" shows how much of any edge trading costs would eat.

    Crypto (experimental). Crypto trades around the clock, so each day is a UTC day: VWAP and the opening range restart at 00:00 UTC. The opening range is the first hour, breakouts are only taken before 06:00 UTC, VWAP reclaims start at 01:00 UTC, and everything is closed by 23:45 UTC. Crypto trades on weekends, can be bought in fractions, and uses its own slippage setting. These are the stock rules re-timed, not separately tuned, and I have not checked how the data feed behaves for crypto, so treat those results as a first look only. The Robinhood connection planned for this project is described as covering stocks, so automating crypto would need a different route.

    Limits of this test. Yahoo only provides about 60 days of 5-minute data, which is a few dozen trades per symbol. That is enough to catch bugs, not enough to prove an edge. Each symbol is tested on its own, so portfolio-wide limits are only approximated. Long only. Earnings days and FOMC/CPI days are not filtered out yet.
